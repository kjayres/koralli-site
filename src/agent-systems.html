POSEIDON / THE ORCHESTRATOR

One direction. Four coordinators.

The planned orchestrator assigns work, selects models and sets compute allowances. The aim is to evaluate those choices against outcomes, cost, response time and feedback from the people using the workspace.

Galene looks after uptime. Nereus covers stability and safe change. Proteus handles labelled roles, scenarios and authorised representations. Triton coordinates communications. Each can bring in specialist subagents.

  1. 01

    Set the task

    Define the question, useful evidence and what a good result would look like.

  2. 02

    Route the work

    Choose specialists and models within the available time and budget.

  3. 03

    Review the result

    Assess the outcome alongside cost, response time and user feedback.

  4. 04

    Learn from it

    Use those observations to test and refine future assignments.

A research direction shown through an interface prototype. The routing and learning system is still to be developed and evaluated.

THE DESIGN PRINCIPLES

Different roles. A shared question.

01

Specialist roles.

Give each agent a defined task, useful tools and a clear scope. The aim is to combine different strengths, with responsibilities that people can understand and change.

02

Shared sources.

Keep the records and definitions close to the work. Findings should lead back to evidence, with gaps and conflicting accounts available for the team to examine.

03

Human judgement.

Make review part of the workflow. People should be able to inspect a result, question its reasoning, request changes and decide which actions to take.

WHERE THE RESEARCH GOES NEXT

Which combinations are useful?

We want to compare combinations of specialists and interchangeable open-weight models on the same tasks, including whether coordination helps enough to justify its cost. Evidence quality, reliability and the effort required from people all matter.

Poseidon gives that question a place in the workspace. Establishing when its choices improve the work remains a research task.
