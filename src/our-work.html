01 / THE KORALLI APPROACH

The question shapes the work.

  1. 1

    UNDERSTAND THE QUESTION

    Go & See

    Start with the people doing the work.

    We listen to stakeholders, follow how work moves through the organisation and examine the records behind it. Together, we identify the decisions that matter and where our understanding is incomplete.

    Research helps us ask a better question and decide what is worth building.

  2. 2

    MAKE SOMETHING USEFUL

    Build & Learn

    Put the thinking in people’s hands.

    A finding might become a measure, a dashboard, a working tool or a different way of organising the work. We build something people can use, examine and challenge.

    Their experience shows us what works, what is missing and what to change. Each version gives us more to learn from.

  3. 3

    LEAVE A CAPABILITY THAT DEVELOPS

    Adapt & Grow

    Give the work somewhere to go next.

    Researchers, operators and your team stay connected as the work develops. We use what we have learnt to refine the capability, explore new questions and decide where to take it next.

    The aim is to leave your team able to use, question and develop what we have built together.

AN ANONYMISED BANK ENGAGEMENT

One picture of delivery.

A global bank wanted to understand the performance of a project portfolio representing more than $100 million in annual investment.

The records were fragmented, and different parts of the organisation measured delivery in different ways. Before a dashboard could be useful, we needed to agree what it should measure.

We worked alongside the bank in two-week sprints, combining stakeholder research, operational experience, data analysis and development. Regular conversations with the people using the work shaped each iteration.

  1. Go & See

    We spoke with stakeholders about the decisions they needed to make, which measures would help and what the data could support. We kept a measure only if a leader could act differently depending on its value.

    Making the project our common unit of measurement gave us a basis for comparing delivery across teams and reconciling records from different systems.

  2. Build & Learn

    We built dashboards that let users move from a portfolio-wide view into the detail behind each measure.

    Users examined the results, challenged their interpretation and identified what was missing. Those conversations shaped the dashboards and the definitions underneath them.

  3. Adapt & Grow

    We deployed the dashboards through the bank’s reporting environment and used them to analyse portfolio performance.

    The data foundation opened up further questions about differences in delivery, recurring patterns and what historical data could tell us about future performance.

Each phase informed the next. Building and using the dashboards brought us back to questions about the data, the measures and what leaders needed to know.

03 / WHAT CHANGED

A foundation to work from.

01

A shared view of delivery.

Leaders could examine performance across products, teams, regions and lines of business, then investigate the projects behind the overall picture.

02

Measures grounded in the records.

The bank’s proposed indicators became measures that could be traced back to source systems, with a named owner for each number. Time and cost of delivery could be understood through shared definitions.

03

A foundation for further research.

The reconciled data supported analysis beyond routine reporting, giving the bank a basis for investigating what influences performance.
