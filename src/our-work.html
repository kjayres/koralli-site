AN ANONYMISED BANK ENGAGEMENT

One picture of delivery.

A global bank wanted to understand the performance of a project portfolio representing more than $100 million in annual investment.

The ambition was clear. How to measure it wasn’t. Records were fragmented, and different parts of the organisation defined delivery in different ways.

Built with the people using it.

We began with the decisions leaders needed to make. A measure was useful only if its value could change a decision. Using the project as a common unit let us reconcile records and compare delivery across teams.

Working in two-week sprints, we built dashboards that connected the portfolio view to individual projects. Users examined the results, challenged their interpretation and helped refine the measures and definitions.

The dashboards were deployed through the bank’s reporting environment, giving the team a shared basis for examining delivery and asking further questions.

A foundation to work from.

A shared view of delivery.

Leaders could move from portfolio performance across products, teams and regions to the projects behind it.

Measures grounded in the records.

Time and cost measures used shared definitions, could be traced to source systems and had a named owner for each number.

A foundation for further research.

Reconciled records gave the bank a basis for investigating patterns in delivery and what might explain them.
